# Whole-park sensor placement (modelled and simulated)

**MODELLED priority surface and line-of-sight visibility; SIMULATED fires and detection times from a simplified, uncalibrated spread model. Not a prediction of real fires or real detection times, and not a siting plan.** Generated by `python -m park.run`; the same numbers are in `results.json`.

## Question

Sensor masts go on any of 8,874 candidate cells of Khao Yai National Park (131 x 187 cells of 500 m, the park plus a 5 km margin). For 10, 20 and 40 sensors, how much of the park do three different placement rules cover, how close is the greedy coverage placement to the best possible, and does the simulated spread of fire change which sites are chosen?

## Method

- Candidates and visibility (modelled): every cell with more than half its area inside the park, a 30 m mast, 4 km range, line of sight over the elevation grid (`park/instance.py`). A site also sees cells in the margin.
- Priority (modelled): hazard x value from the project's hand formula, rescaled over the whole grid. Margin cells carry a value of 0.1 instead of 0.5 to 1.0, so they still have weight.
- Placements, all by greedy selection on the same visibility matrix (`park/solvers.py`):
  - Priority coverage: maximise the priority weight of the cells seen, over the whole grid (the project's existing objective at park scale).
  - Fire-history coverage: maximise the ignition weight of the cells seen. Ignition weights are the smoothed 2012 to 2025 satellite fire counts (log, Gaussian, sigma = 1 cell), restricted to cells inside the park.
  - Time-to-detection, greedy: maximise the expected hours saved against the satellite on 2,000 TRAIN scenarios (seed 101) with `detection.solvers.greedy_detection`. No exact solver is run at this scale.
- Scoring (the same function for every placement): the share of in-park priority weight, in-park area and 2012 to 2025 in-park fire detections that fall in cells the sensors see; and, on 2,000 independent TEST scenarios (seed 202), the mean detection time and the hours saved against satellite only.
- Simulated fires (`detection/fire_sim.py`): ignitions are drawn only in cells inside the park, in proportion to the ignition weights; fire spreads over the whole grid. About 12 times more detections lie in the surrounding margin than in the park (15,101 against 1,264); fires that start there and spread in are not modelled as ignitions. One daytime wind value per scenario, drawn from the fire-season record.
- Detection: a sensor detects a fire the moment it enters a cell it sees; the satellite detects every fire 6 hours after ignition (assumption). Headline spread rate r0 = 2 m/min. A scenario's detection time is the earlier of the two.

## Finding

The two placements share 3 to 9 of 20 sites (0 of 9 settings are identical), so at this scale the spread model does change which sites are chosen. It does not clearly improve detection: on TEST the fire-history placement detects earlier in 6 of 9 settings, the timed placement in 1, and 2 are inside the bootstrap interval. The difference in mean detection time (timed minus fire-history) runs from -0.193 to +0.221 h.

The timed placement is chosen on a sample of simulated ignitions, while fire-history coverage uses the full smoothed ignition distribution, so part of the timed placement's shortfall may be sampling noise in that choice. This was not tested.

The settings reuse the same TRAIN and TEST seeds, so they are not independent confirmations.

## Limitations

- Every in-park cell is a candidate site, with no account of road access, land tenure, power or maintenance. The result is a geometric comparison, not a siting plan.
- Visibility is line of sight over 500 m cells from a 30 m mast. It includes no tree-canopy height, smoke or weather. The rule rounds ray positions to cells in a way that depends on a site's absolute row and column parity, so about 6% of viewshed cells differ from the 20 x 20 window's results for the same sites.
- Priority terms are rescaled over the whole park, so cell values differ slightly from the small window's (correlation 0.996).
- The spread rate comes from experimental burns in deciduous forest elsewhere in Thailand. The land-cover data shows the park as 98% tree cover without separating evergreen from deciduous forest. The spread model is uncalibrated.
- Fires spread slowly in this model (a tree-cover cell burns at 1.2 m/min before slope and wind at the headline rate), so detection at ignition inside a seen cell supplies most of the sensor detections in the table. Instant detection on entering a seen cell favours sensors more than real detection would.
- Ignitions are drawn inside the park only. About 12 times more detections lie in the margin, and fires spreading in from outside are not simulated.
- The fire-history and time-to-detection placements are chosen and scored on the same fire-history distribution, which is circular; TRAIN and TEST separate only the random scenarios. The hazard layer's fire term comes from the same detections.
- The priority-coverage placement also weighs what is worth protecting, while the other two optimise ignitions only. That the placements score differently on each other's measures does not rank them, and no winner is declared between objectives.
- The satellite baseline is an assumption, not a measurement. Differences between placements inside a bootstrap interval are not distinguishable at this number of scenarios.
