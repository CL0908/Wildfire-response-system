# Learned hazard model: validation against the hand formula and persistence

Generated by `data/fit_hazard_model.py` on 13 November 2026 (rerun to reproduce; seeds are fixed). Machine-readable numbers: `data/hazard_validation.json`.

## What was tested

Can a fitted model rank 500 m cells by where satellites will detect fires next better than the project's hand-weighted hazard formula, or better than simply using where it burned before? Every method saw fire detections from 2012 to 2019 only and was scored on 2020 to 2024.

- **Persistence** is the where-it-burned-before score: the smoothed count of earlier detections in and around each cell.
- **Capture at 20%** is the share of later detections that fall in the 20% of cells a method ranks highest; a random ranking captures 20%.
- **Average precision** and **ROC AUC** are standard ranking scores, higher is better; a random ranking scores 0.5 on ROC AUC.
- "Clearly" means the 95% interval of the difference excludes zero.

## Answer

**The logistic models do not beat persistence (2020 to 2024). Over the whole grid they tie it; in the park they are clearly worse on average precision and not clearly different on capture at 20%.** Gradient boosting against persistence: whole grid clearly better on ROC AUC; not clearly different on average precision and capture at 20%; park not clearly different on ROC AUC and average precision and capture at 20%.

## Methods compared

- Random: expected values, for reference.
- Hand formula: `priority_surface._real_layers` (0.40 fire density, 0.25 fuel, 0.20 dryness, 0.15 terrain spread, lines 147 to 177), run on this grid. Its fire term uses 2012 to 2019 detections only. The production pipeline uses all years, which would include the test period, so this is the fair version, not the one shipped.
- Persistence (8 years): Gaussian-smoothed (sigma 1 cell) log of 1 plus the 2012 to 2019 count.
- Persistence, 2016 to 2019 only: the same smoothing on four years, the history length the primary learned model gets.
- Logistic, static features: 17 terrain, land-cover, park and distance features, fitted on the 2012 to 2019 label.
- Logistic plus fire history (primary): the same features plus smoothed log count of detections over a history window. Fitted with history 2012 to 2015 against label 2016 to 2019, scored with history 2016 to 2019. This is the primary specification; a variant scored with 2012 to 2019 rescaled to a four-year rate is reported beside it.
- Gradient boosting: same inputs as the primary model, only to check whether non-linearity matters. Not exported.
- Regularisation strength was chosen by 5-fold cross-validation on 20 x 20-cell blocks inside the training period only. No fire detections after 2019 enter any feature or score (see Limits for the land-cover and road data).

## Exported models

`validated` is model e exactly as tested. `deployment` has the same specification but is refitted with history 2012 to 2018 against label 2019 to 2025, so it uses the later fires; it is given the whole 2012 to 2025 record rescaled to seven years when predicting. The split in the test cannot be repeated for 2019 to 2025 because there is nothing left to test on, so this model is not itself validated out of sample; it inherits the validation of the specification. `static` is model d.

## Limits

- Detections are not ignitions. VIIRS misses small and under-canopy fires and sees an overpass a few times a day. Outside the forest it probably sees mostly agricultural burning, so the models likely learn where satellites see fires, a mixture of farm burning and wildfire that was not separated here.
- Inside the park there are few events: 620 of 11,360 test detections fall on park cells, and 6.0% of park cells have any (grid: 17.4%). Park intervals are wide.
- One test period. 2020 alone holds 3,622 of the 11,360 test detections; results with it excluded are shown above. 7,738 detections remain.
- Possible leakage from non-fire data. Land cover is ESA WorldCover 2021, inside the test window, and burn scars can change a cell's class; roads and settlements are an OpenStreetMap snapshot from November 2026. This could leak a little test-period information into the static features. That can only help the hand formula and the learned models, never persistence, which uses fire counts alone, so the comparison is conservative against persistence.
- The hand formula is normalised here over the 200 x 200 grid; production normalises over the 40 x 40 region. On the 40 x 40 region its average precision is 0.426 with grid normalisation and 0.439 with region normalisation (capture at 20%: 38.3% and 38.9%), against 0.473 (41.0%) for persistence, so the order is unchanged.
- The bootstrap intervals resample test cells only. The models were fitted once, so the intervals do not include model-fitting uncertainty.
- Cells are spatially correlated; the block bootstrap accounts for that at 10 km, not beyond. Results come from one area (Khao Yai and surroundings) and may not transfer.
- 2025 (1,503 detections in the grid) is a partial year: excluded from testing, included in the deployment model's label.
- The output ranks cells by the chance of at least one detection over a few years. It is not a calibrated ignition probability, and it says nothing about fire spread or damage.
- Coefficients are associations, not causes.
- No quantum content or advantage is involved in this analysis.
